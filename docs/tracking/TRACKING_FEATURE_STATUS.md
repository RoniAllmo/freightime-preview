# Tracking Feature Status

## Current status: removed, not planned

**Shipment tracking is not part of the active FreighTime product.** The "Single-input tracking router" — the feature that detected carrier tracking numbers (container numbers, AWB numbers, UPS `1Z`/`1R`, UPU S10/EMS) and linked out to official carrier tracking pages — **was removed from the public product in full**, by product-owner decision. This is recorded in `CLAUDE.md` §1 and `IMPORT_READINESS_V1.md` §10, which state:

> The "Single-input tracking router" / tracking search ... has been **removed from the public product in full**. FreighTime cannot return real operational tracking data, so a validate-only utility was judged not valuable enough to keep public.

**No reintroduction of tracking is currently planned.** This document is a current-state statement, not a roadmap, and it does not schedule, promise, or suggest any future restoration. Any future work in this area would require a **new** product decision, made explicitly and separately from this document.

## What this means in practice

- The historical documents this project preserves — `docs/tracking/HISTORICAL_TRACKING_ARCHITECTURE.md` and `docs/tracking/TRACKING_EVIDENCE_MATRIX.md` — describe a **removed** feature and **do not describe current runtime functionality**. Nothing in either document is implemented in the live product today.
- **No code should reintroduce tracking detection, tracking links, or any tracking-adjacent capability without new, explicit product-owner authorization.** This includes container-number detection, AWB detection, UPU S10/EMS detection, any commercial-courier detection (UPS, DSV, DHL, FedEx, Aramex, or any other), any "official tracking page" link, and any live-tracking API integration.
- **FCL / ocean-container live tracking is explicitly included in this removed-feature scope.** It is not a carve-out or an exception — the FCL/ocean-tracking design (`FCL_CONTAINER_TRACKING_DESIGN.md`) was never implemented, and its removal-adjacent status is identical to every other tracking capability described in the historical documents. Nothing about FCL tracking's specific carrier research (MSC/ZIM/Maersk) elevates it above any other deferred or removed tracking work.
- The existing local container/AWB/S10 structural detectors and their tests, described historically, no longer exist in this repository's runtime code; they are documented only for the historical record.

## What any future reintroduction would require

Should the product owner decide, at some future point and through a separate, explicit decision, to reintroduce any tracking capability, the historical documents make clear that at minimum the following would be needed — none of which exists today:

1. **A new product decision**, made explicitly for that purpose. The prior removal decision is not reversed by anything in this document, and nothing here should be read as anticipating or preparing for that decision.
2. **A threat/privacy review.** The removed feature's own design record repeatedly treated tracking identifiers as potentially sensitive operational data and imposed strict rules against placing them in URLs, logs, or persistent storage. Any future work would need its own current privacy and threat assessment, not an assumption that the old rules still fully cover a new implementation.
3. **Fresh carrier evidence.** The carrier and identifier evidence recorded in `TRACKING_EVIDENCE_MATRIX.md` is a point-in-time snapshot from the original research phase. Official carrier APIs, formats, developer-portal terms, and access models can change; none of that evidence should be treated as current without being re-verified.
4. **New testing.** No tracking-related test coverage exists in the current codebase. Any future implementation would need its own test suite built against the current codebase and current evidence, not a reuse of historical test claims.

### Documents with no unique content worth preserving beyond their evidence row

None of the 14 source documents were found to be a full duplicate of another with zero unique content. Two pairs of documents have substantial overlap where a later document supersedes an earlier one on the *same specific claim* (noted in the "omitted" column above): `COURIER_EMS_RESEARCH.md`'s original EMS-range finding is superseded by `EMS_CLASSIFICATION_RESEARCH.md`'s primary-source confirmation, and `S10_AUTHORITATIVE_VERIFICATION.md`'s earlier "not confirmed" check-digit boundary status is superseded by its own later "Manual boundary-case verification" section within the same document. In both cases the superseding document's conclusion — not the superseded interim finding — is what was carried into Docs 1–2. Each of the five individual courier-research documents (Aramex, DHL, DSV, FedEx, UPS) contributes a distinct evidence row to `TRACKING_EVIDENCE_MATRIX.md`; none is redundant with another, since each concerns a different carrier.
